## The order to study in

Not 1 → 13. Recommended sequence, with reasoning:

1. **Ch 3, 4** (Algebra) — mechanical, fast, 10 marks banked early. Good confidence-builder.
2. **Ch 5** (Continuity & Differentiability) — the foundation for Ch 6, and for Physics.
3. **Ch 6** (Application of Derivatives) — depends on Ch 5.
4. **Ch 7** (Integrals) — the largest chapter; give it 4–5 weeks, not less.
5. **Ch 8, 9** (Application of Integrals, Differential Equations) — both depend on Ch 7.
6. **Ch 10, 11** (Vectors, 3D) — self-contained, formula-heavy, 14 marks for modest effort.
7. **Ch 13, 12** (Probability, LP) — self-contained, 13 marks, both small.
8. **Ch 1, 2** (Relations & Functions, Inverse Trig) — abstract, and small after deletions. Last.

The only hard dependencies are 5 → 6 and 7 → {8, 9}. Everything else can be reordered to fit school.

## Three habits specific to Maths

**1. Keep a "standard results" page.** Not a formula sheet — a page of results you derived once and
should never derive again: ∫sec x dx, ∫dx/(x²−a²), the derivative of tan⁻¹((1−x)/(1+x)), the area of
a quadrant of an ellipse. Add to it as you go; read it weekly.

**2. Write one step per line.** Two algebraic moves on one line is how sign errors get made and how
method marks get lost. This costs paper and saves marks.

**3. After every wrong answer, write down *which line* was wrong.** Not "I got it wrong" — "line 4,
I dropped the negative sign when transposing". That sentence is what stops it recurring, and it is
what goes in the error log in [`../docs/study-plan.md`](../docs/study-plan.md).
