## Units I + II — Electrostatics and Current Electricity (16 marks)

### Ch 1. Electric Charges and Fields

| Topic | Marks | Freq | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gauss's law: statement, then apply to infinite wire / infinite sheet / spherical shell | 3–5 | Every year | **P1** |
| Electric field on the axial and equatorial line of a dipole | 3–5 | 8/10 | **P1** |
| Torque on a dipole in a uniform field; τ = p × E | 2–3 | 8/10 | **P1** |
| Electric flux through a surface / through a cube face | 1–2 | 9/10 | **P1** |
| Coulomb's law, and force on a charge from two or three others | 2–3 | 7/10 | P2 |
| Electric field lines: properties, and sketching for given charges | 2 | 7/10 | P2 |
| Superposition — net field at a point | 2–3 | 7/10 | P2 |
| Field inside a conductor is zero; charge resides on the surface | 1–2 | 6/10 | P2 |
| Continuous charge distribution, linear/surface/volume charge density | 1 | 5/10 | P2 |

**Pattern note.** Gauss's law is the anchor of this chapter and reappears in three costumes: a long
straight wire (E = λ/2πε₀r), an infinite plane sheet (E = σ/2ε₀), and a uniformly charged thin
spherical shell (E = q/4πε₀r² outside, **zero inside**). The shell's "zero inside" result is asked
as an MCQ or Assertion–Reason almost every year on its own.

**The trap.** Writing the Gaussian surface without justifying *why* that surface (symmetry, and E
parallel/perpendicular to the area vector). That justification is a mark.

| Relation E = −dV/dr | 1–2 | 7/10 | P2 |

| Dielectric polarisation, dielectric constant | 1–2 | 5/10 | P2 |

**Pattern note.** The "insert a dielectric" question is worth singling out. There are two cases and
CBSE asks them interchangeably: **battery still connected** (V constant, so C↑, Q↑, U↑) versus
**battery disconnected** (Q constant, so C↑, V↓, U↓). Students memorise one case and answer the
other. Build a small table for both and learn it as a table.

Van de Graaff generator is deleted.

### Ch 3. Current Electricity

| Topic | Marks | Freq | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Drift velocity; derive I = neAv_d and hence Ohm's law | 3–5 | Every year | **P1** |
| Resistivity, and its dependence on temperature (conductor vs semiconductor) | 2–3 | 9/10 | **P1** |
| Cells in series and parallel; equivalent emf and internal resistance | 3 | 8/10 | **P1** |
| Kirchhoff's rules: statement, and apply to a two-loop circuit | 3–5 | 9/10 | **P1** |
| Wheatstone bridge: balance condition, derived by Kirchhoff | 3–5 | 8/10 | **P1** |
| Metre bridge: circuit, working, finding unknown R | 3 | 6/10 | P2 |
| Internal resistance from a V–I graph, or terminal potential difference | 2–3 | 8/10 | **P1** |
| Mobility; relation between current density, conductivity and E | 1–2 | 7/10 | P2 |
| Relaxation time; effect on resistance of stretching a wire | 2 | 6/10 | P2 |
| Power dissipated; maximum power transfer | 2 | 5/10 | P2 |
| Non-ohmic conductors and V–I graphs | 1–2 | 5/10 | P2 |

**Pattern note.** The **potentiometer is fully deleted**, theory and practical. That removed a
guaranteed 3–5 mark question and its weight went to Kirchhoff/Wheatstone and to drift velocity. If a
practice paper asks you to compare two cells' emf with a potentiometer, it is an old paper.

**The trap.** In the drift-velocity derivation, students write I = neAv_d and stop. The question asks
you to *hence* obtain Ohm's law — you must substitute v_d = (eE/m)τ and E = V/l to reach
R = ml/(ne²Aτ). The last step is where the marks are.

**Pattern note.** LC oscillations are deleted. The series LCR question is close to guaranteed and is
usually the 5-marker for this block — phasor diagram, impedance expression, then a numerical or the
resonance condition. Draw the phasor diagram even when it is not explicitly asked; it is worth a
mark on its own.

**The trap.** Writing average power as V_rms I_rms and omitting cos φ. And, for a purely inductive or
capacitive circuit, not stating that the average power over a cycle is **zero**.

## Units V + VI — Electromagnetic Waves and Optics (18 marks) — the largest block

### Ch 8. Electromagnetic Waves

| Topic | Marks | Freq | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Displacement current: what it is, why Ampere's law needed modification | 2–3 | 8/10 | **P1** |
| EM spectrum: order, wavelength ranges, sources and uses | 2–3 | 8/10 | **P1** |
| Properties of EM waves (transverse, E ⊥ B ⊥ direction, speed c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀)) | 2–3 | 8/10 | **P1** |

| Energy density of an EM wave | 1–2 | 4/10 | P3 |

**Pattern note.** A small chapter that reliably supplies 2–4 marks, mostly in Sections A and B. It is
the best marks-per-page ratio in the book. Make a one-page EM-spectrum table (radio → microwave →
IR → visible → UV → X-ray → gamma, with wavelength range, source, detector, use) and learn it cold —
that table alone answers most of what is asked here.

**Pattern note.** Ray Optics alone typically carries **8–10 marks**, and it is the one chapter that
appears in every section of the paper — MCQ, 2-mark, 3-mark derivation, case study, and 5-mark
instrument question. It is the highest-priority chapter in Physics, full stop.

**The trap — sign convention.** More marks are lost to sign convention in this chapter than to
anything else in the paper. Fix the rule once: distances measured from the pole/optical centre, in
the direction of incident light positive, against it negative; heights above the axis positive. Write
`u = −30 cm` explicitly rather than substituting 30 and hoping.

**The trap — ray diagrams.** They must show at least two rays, arrowheads on every ray, the image
clearly located, and all of F, 2F, O and C labelled. The diagram is worth 1–2 of the 5 marks on the
instrument questions and is marked independently of the algebra.

## Units VII + VIII — Dual Nature, Atoms and Nuclei (12 marks)

### Ch 11. Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter

| Topic | Marks | Freq | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Einstein's photoelectric equation; explain the observed laws with it | 3–4 | Every year | **P1** |
| Graphs: stopping potential vs frequency; photocurrent vs potential for different intensities | 2–3 | 9/10 | **P1** |
| Work function, threshold frequency/wavelength; numericals | 2–3 | 9/10 | **P1** |
| de Broglie wavelength; λ = h/p, and λ = h/√(2meV) for an accelerated electron | 2–3 | 9/10 | **P1** |
| Laws of photoelectric emission | 2–3 | 8/10 | **P1** |
| Why the wave theory fails to explain the photoelectric effect | 2 | 6/10 | P2 |
| Photon: energy, momentum, and its properties | 1–2 | 7/10 | P2 |
| Davisson–Germer experiment (qualitative) | 1–2 | 4/10 | P3 |

**Pattern note.** Highly formulaic and therefore high-return — one equation
(hν = φ₀ + ½mv²_max = φ₀ + eV₀) plus two graphs generates nearly every question. Case-study
questions in Section D draw on this chapter often, using the graphs as the "source".

**What the graph question actually wants:** for stopping potential V₀ vs frequency ν, the slope is
**h/e** and the x-intercept is the **threshold frequency**; the line's slope is the *same* for all
metals and only the intercept changes. That is the asked fact.

## Unit IX — Electronic Devices (7 marks)

### Ch 14. Semiconductor Electronics

| Topic | Marks | Freq | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

**Pattern note.** **Read this even if you read nothing else on this page.** Zener diode, LED,
photodiode, solar cell, transistors and logic gates are all deleted. What is left is five topics —
and it still carries **7 marks**, a full 10% of the paper.

That makes Ch 14 the highest marks-per-hour chapter in Physics by a wide margin. Students skip it
because it is the last chapter in the book and because they remember it as "the hard transistor
chapter" from seniors. It no longer is. Two focused days here is worth more than two weeks on
Magnetism and Matter.

**The rectifier question specifics.** You need: the circuit diagram (with the transformer and load
resistance shown), the input waveform, and the output waveform, correctly aligned. For the full-wave
rectifier, show that output pulses appear for **both** half-cycles. The three diagrams are the bulk
of the marks; the explanation is one or two lines.

## Putting it together: a 30-day Physics triage list

Every **P1** row above. In chapter order:

- **Ch 1:** Gauss's law + three applications; dipole field; flux
- **Ch 2:** parallel-plate capacitor ± dielectric; energy stored; series/parallel; potential; equipotentials
- **Ch 3:** drift velocity → Ohm's law; resistivity vs temperature; Kirchhoff; Wheatstone; cells; internal resistance
- **Ch 4:** Biot–Savart for a loop; Ampere's law → long straight wire; galvanometer conversion; F = I(l×B); F = q(v×B)
- **Ch 5:** Earth's magnetic elements only
- **Ch 6:** Faraday and Lenz; motional emf; self-inductance; AC generator; direction of induced current
- **Ch 7:** series LCR + phasor; resonance and Q; transformer; power factor; pure L and pure C
- **Ch 8:** displacement current; EM spectrum table; properties of EM waves
- **Ch 9 (do this first):** prism formula; lens maker's formula; mirror formula; TIR; power and combination; microscope; telescope; sign-convention numericals
- **Ch 10:** YDSE fringe width; single-slit diffraction; conditions for interference; effect of changing λ/D/d
- **Ch 11:** Einstein's equation; the two graphs; work function numericals; de Broglie
- **Ch 12:** Bohr's postulates; E_n = −13.6/n²; spectral series; energy level diagram
- **Ch 13:** BE/nucleon curve; mass defect numericals; fission and fusion; R = R₀A^(1/3)
- **Ch 14:** the whole chapter

That is roughly 45–50 of 70 marks, and Ch 9 + Ch 14 + Ch 7 alone are about 25 of them.
