# Study Plan — Maths + Physics, Class 12 CBSE

Two plans. Pick by how much time is left.

- **Plan A — Full session (April → February).** The one to follow if you are starting on time.
- **Plan B — 100 days.** If you are starting in November/December.
- **Plan C — 30 days.** Triage. Read the honesty note before using it.

All plans assume board exams in **late February / March**, with pre-boards in **December/January**.

---

## The two rules that matter more than the plan

**Rule 1 — Learn a chapter, then immediately do its PYQs.** Not "finish the syllabus, then start
PYQs in January". A chapter you learnt in June and first tested in January is a chapter you will
learn twice. Every chapter note in this repo has its PYQ set attached for exactly this reason.

**Rule 2 — Revise on a schedule, not on a feeling.** After finishing a chapter, revisit it at
**day 7** and **day 30** — 20 minutes each time, formula box plus two hard PYQs. Two short revisits
beat one long re-read, by a wide margin. Keep a table:

| Chapter | Learnt | Day-7 revisit | Day-30 revisit | Test score |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M-05 Continuity & Diff. | 12 Jun | 19 Jun | 12 Jul | 82% |

That table is your actual study plan. Everything below is just the first column.

## Plan A — Full session

### Sequencing logic

Maths and Physics are studied **in parallel**, not one then the other, because school teaches them
in parallel and because Physics needs calculus you learn in Maths.

Two ordering choices are deliberate:

- **Maths starts with Matrices/Determinants, not Chapter 1.** Relations & Functions is abstract and
  is a poor confidence-builder in April. Algebra is mechanical, scores fast, and is only 10 marks —
  finish it early and it never worries you again.
- **Physics follows book order (Electrostatics → Semiconductors).** Physics genuinely builds:
  potential needs field, EMI needs magnetism, AC needs EMI. Don't reorder Physics.

### Term 1: April → September (finish 80% of syllabus)

| Weeks | Maths | Physics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Apr, wk 1–2** | Ch 3 Matrices · Ch 4 Determinants | Ch 1 Electric Charges and Fields |
| **Apr, wk 3–4** | Ch 1 Relations and Functions · Ch 2 Inverse Trig | Ch 2 Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance |
| **May, wk 1–2** | Ch 5 Continuity and Differentiability | Ch 2 (cont.) · Ch 3 Current Electricity |
| **May, wk 3–4** | Ch 5 (cont.) · Ch 6 Application of Derivatives | Ch 3 (cont.) — **Unit I+II done (16 marks)** |
| **Jun, wk 1–2** | Ch 6 (cont.) | Ch 4 Moving Charges and Magnetism |

### Weekly rhythm inside Plan A

Assume ~3 hours/day on these two subjects (the rest of the day belongs to your other three subjects).

| Day | Maths | Physics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | 1 h new topic | 1 h new topic + 30 min numericals |
| Tue | 1 h new topic + 30 min PYQs | 1 h new topic |

Sunday off is part of the plan, not a failure of it.

## Plan B — 100 days (starting ~mid-November)

You cannot cover everything at equal depth. Cover everything at *unequal* depth, deliberately.

### Days 1–45: high-weightage core

**Maths (in this order):**
1. Ch 7 Integrals (the single largest source of marks in the paper)
2. Ch 5 Continuity and Differentiability
3. Ch 3 Matrices + Ch 4 Determinants (fast, mechanical, 10 marks)
4. Ch 6 Application of Derivatives
5. Ch 10 Vector Algebra + Ch 11 3D Geometry (14 marks, very formula-driven — high return)
6. Ch 9 Differential Equations
7. Ch 13 Probability + Ch 12 Linear Programming (13 marks, both small)
8. Ch 8 Application of Integrals
9. Ch 1 + Ch 2 (8 marks, mostly MCQ-level after deletions)

**Physics (in this order):**
1. Ch 9 Ray Optics (largest single chapter)
2. Ch 3 Current Electricity
3. Ch 2 Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance
4. Ch 7 Alternating Current + Ch 6 EMI
5. Ch 4 Moving Charges and Magnetism
6. Ch 10 Wave Optics
7. Ch 14 Semiconductor Electronics (7 marks, tiny syllabus — do not skip)
8. Ch 11 Dual Nature + Ch 12 Atoms + Ch 13 Nuclei
9. Ch 1 Electric Charges and Fields
10. Ch 5 Magnetism and Matter + Ch 8 EM Waves (small after deletions, mostly 1–2 markers)

For each chapter: *Brief* → formula box → *PYQs with solutions* → *Test yourself*. Skip nothing
inside a chapter you have decided to do; a half-learnt chapter scores worse than a skipped one
because it eats the same time.

### Days 46–75: PYQs

Last 10 years, chapter-wise first (all Integrals questions together, all Optics questions together —
pattern recognition builds far faster this way), then 6 full papers per subject in chronological order.

### Days 76–100: mocks and the error log

One full paper every alternate day, alternating subjects. Score it yourself, harshly. Every dropped
mark goes in the error log. Final week: error log + formula sheets only.

## Plan C — 30 days (triage)

**Be honest about what this is.** In 30 days you are playing for a solid pass and a decent
percentage, not for 95. That is a perfectly reasonable goal, and the way to hit it is ruthless
prioritisation — not trying to do everything badly.

### The 60% rule

Roughly 60% of both papers comes from a minority of the syllabus. Target that, completely.

**Maths — the 30-day list (~55 of 80 marks):**

| Do completely | Marks reachable |
| --- | --- |
| Ch 7 Integrals: standard forms, substitution, by parts, partial fractions, definite-integral properties | ~12 |
| Ch 3 + Ch 4: inverse of 3×3, solving equations by matrix method, adjoint, properties | ~10 |
| Ch 10 + Ch 11: dot/cross product, projection, angle, line equations, shortest distance | ~14 |
| Ch 13 Probability: conditional probability, total probability, Bayes | ~8 |
| Ch 12 Linear Programming: graphical method, corner-point rule | ~5 |
| Ch 5: continuity at a point, differentiability, chain rule, implicit, logarithmic | ~6 |

**Physics — the 30-day list (~45 of 70 marks):**

| Do completely | Marks reachable |
| --- | --- |
| Ch 9 Ray Optics: mirror/lens formula, refraction, prism, TIR, microscope + telescope | ~10 |
| Ch 3 Current Electricity: Ohm's law, drift velocity, resistivity, cells in series/parallel, Kirchhoff, Wheatstone | ~7 |
| Ch 2 Potential & Capacitance: potential due to charge/dipole, equipotentials, capacitance, dielectric, energy | ~6 |
| Ch 7 AC: LCR series, impedance, resonance, power factor, transformer | ~6 |
| Ch 14 Semiconductors: whole chapter | ~7 |
| Ch 10 Wave Optics: YDSE fringe width, single-slit diffraction, polarisation | ~5 |
| Ch 11 Dual Nature: photoelectric effect, Einstein's equation, de Broglie | ~4 |

### 30-day shape

- **Days 1–18:** the lists above, roughly one chapter per 2 days, brief + formulas + PYQs.
- **Days 19–25:** last 5 years' PYQs, chapter-wise, restricted to the topics you studied.
- **Days 26–29:** two full mocks per subject. Attempt *everything*, including topics you skipped —
  MCQs from unstudied chapters are often guessable, and Assertion–Reason often is too.
- **Day 30:** formula sheets. Sleep 8 hours.

### What you are giving up

Relations & Functions proofs, Application of Integrals, Differential Equations beyond the linear
form, Magnetism and Matter, Atoms and Nuclei derivations, EM Waves detail. You will lose marks
there. Attempt their MCQs anyway.

## The error log — the highest-return habit in this document

One page per subject, maintained from day one. For every mark you drop in any test:

| Date | Q | Chapter | What went wrong | Category |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 14 Jan | 32 | M-07 Integrals | Forgot to change limits after substitution | **Method** |
| 14 Jan | 28 | P-09 Optics | Sign convention: took u positive | **Concept** |
| 16 Jan | 21 | M-04 Determinants | Arithmetic slip in cofactor | **Careless** |
| 16 Jan | 33 | P-07 AC | Didn't draw the phasor diagram | **Presentation** |

Four categories, and each has a different fix:

- **Concept** — go back to the chapter *Brief*. This is the only category that needs re-learning.
- **Method** — you knew the concept but not the standard procedure. Drill 5 more of the same type.
- **Careless** — arithmetic, transcription, sign slips. Fix by *slowing down the first line* of every
  question, not by working more problems.
- **Presentation** — missing diagram, missing unit, no final statement, unlabelled graph. Free
  marks. Fix by re-reading section 4 of the [blueprint](00-exam-blueprint.md) once a week.

In the last two weeks, count your categories. If most entries are *Careless* or *Presentation*, you
do not need more study — you need slower, more disciplined writing. Most students in February get
this diagnosis exactly backwards and study more when they should be writing more carefully.

## Practical / internal assessment — 20 (Maths) + 30 (Physics)

Fifty marks, and they are the easiest fifty in the year. Do not treat them as an afterthought.

- **Physics practical file:** write it up as you go, not the week before. Each experiment needs aim,
  apparatus, theory with formula, diagram, observation table, calculation, result with error, and
  precautions. The diagram and the observation table are where marks are actually lost.
- **Physics viva (5 marks):** the examiner asks about the *theory* of your experiments — least count,
  why zero error matters, the formula's origin. Know the theory of every experiment in your file.
- **Physics investigatory project (3 marks):** choose something you can explain in two minutes.
- **Maths portfolio and activities (10 marks):** neatness and completeness are the whole mark. Hand
  it in on time.
- **Periodic tests (Maths, 10 marks):** these are averaged. A bad September test costs you board
  percentage. Take each one seriously.
