# Exam Blueprint — Maths (041) and Physics (042)

Read this once, properly, before opening a single chapter. Almost every avoidable mark lost in the
board exam is lost because the student did not know how the paper is built.

---

## 1. Mathematics (Code 041)

**Theory 80 marks · Internal assessment 20 marks · 3 hours**

### Unit-wise weightage (official)

| Unit | Chapters | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I. Relations and Functions | 1. Relations and Functions · 2. Inverse Trigonometric Functions | 8 |
| II. Algebra | 3. Matrices · 4. Determinants | 10 |
| III. Calculus | 5. Continuity & Differentiability · 6. Application of Derivatives · 7. Integrals · 8. Application of Integrals · 9. Differential Equations | **35** |
| IV. Vectors and Three-Dimensional Geometry | 10. Vector Algebra · 11. Three Dimensional Geometry | 14 |
| V. Linear Programming | 12. Linear Programming | 5 |
| VI. Probability | 13. Probability | 8 |
| | **Total** | **80** |

The CBSE syllabus document carries an explicit line: *"No chapter-wise weightage. Care to be taken
to cover all chapters."* So the numbers above are **unit** totals, and the split between chapters
inside a unit shifts year to year. Do not use "chapter-wise weightage" tables from coaching sites as
a reason to skip a chapter — Application of Integrals has appeared as a 5-marker in most years
despite being "only 4 marks" on such tables.

### Paper design

38 questions, five sections, no overall choice (internal choice in Sections B–E).

| Section | Questions | Type | Marks each | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 1–18 | MCQ, one correct option | 1 | 18 |
| A | 19–20 | Assertion–Reason | 1 | 2 |
| B | 21–25 | Very Short Answer (VSA) | 2 | 10 |
| C | 26–31 | Short Answer (SA) | 3 | 18 |
| D | 32–35 | Long Answer (LA) | 5 | 20 |
| E | 36–38 | Case-study / source-based | 4 | 12 |
| | **38** | | | **80** |

### Internal assessment (20)

| Component | Marks |
| --- | --- |
| Periodic tests | 10 |
| Mathematics activities / lab | 5 |
| Portfolio (assignments, project work) | 5 |

### Where the 5-markers come from

Over the last decade, the four Section-D questions have almost always been drawn from this pool.
Prepare **all** of it; you will then be able to pick your best four.

- Matrix method / inverse of a 3×3 matrix to solve a system of three equations
- Definite or indefinite integral (partial fractions, or a by-parts + substitution combination)
- Area bounded by curves (line with parabola / circle / ellipse)
- Differential equation — linear form, or homogeneous
- Maxima–minima word problem (volume, area, cost, distance)
- Shortest distance between two skew lines / image of a point in a line
- Relations and functions: prove one-one and onto
- Probability: Bayes' theorem (occasionally placed in Section D instead of C)

### Where the 4-mark case studies come from

Three case studies, and historically they cluster hard:

1. **Probability** — a Bayes / total-probability story (factories, machines, students, diseases).
2. **Application of derivatives** — a maxima–minima story (a box, a window, a wire cut in two, cost).
3. **Linear programming, 3D geometry, or a differential-equation growth/decay story** — this third
   slot is the variable one.

Each case study has 2–3 parts, usually `2 + 2` or `1 + 1 + 2`, with an internal choice in the last part.

## 2. Physics (Code 042)

**Theory 70 marks · Practical 30 marks · 3 hours**

### Unit-wise weightage (official)

CBSE publishes Physics weightage in **paired units**. That pairing is itself information: it tells
you the board balances marks across the pair, not chapter by chapter.

| Units | Chapters | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I. Electrostatics + II. Current Electricity | 1. Electric Charges and Fields · 2. Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance · 3. Current Electricity | 16 |
| III. Magnetic Effects of Current and Magnetism + IV. Electromagnetic Induction and Alternating Currents | 4. Moving Charges and Magnetism · 5. Magnetism and Matter · 6. Electromagnetic Induction · 7. Alternating Current | 17 |
| V. Electromagnetic Waves + VI. Optics | 8. Electromagnetic Waves · 9. Ray Optics and Optical Instruments · 10. Wave Optics | **18** |
| VII. Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter + VIII. Atoms and Nuclei | 11. Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter · 12. Atoms · 13. Nuclei | 12 |
| IX. Electronic Devices | 14. Semiconductor Electronics | 7 |
| | **Total** | **70** |

Two things follow immediately:

- **Optics is the largest single block.** Ray Optics alone typically carries ~10 of those 18 marks.
  It is the last chapter you should ever leave incomplete.
- **Semiconductor Electronics is 7 marks from one short chapter with a small syllabus.** It is the
  highest marks-per-hour chapter in the paper. Students skip it because it is last in the book;
  that is a free 7 marks handed away.

### Paper design

33 questions, five sections, no overall choice (internal choice in Sections B–E).

| Section | Questions | Type | Marks each | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 1–12 | MCQ | 1 | 12 |
| A | 13–16 | Assertion–Reason | 1 | 4 |
| B | 17–21 | Very Short Answer | 2 | 10 |
| C | 22–28 | Short Answer | 3 | 21 |
| D | 29–30 | Case-study based | 4 | 8 |
| E | 31–33 | Long Answer | 5 | 15 |
| | **33** | | | **70** |

### Practical / internal assessment (30)

| Component | Marks |
| --- | --- |
| Two experiments (one from each section) | 7 + 7 = 14 |
| Practical record (experiments + activities) | 5 |
| One activity from any section | 3 |
| Investigatory project | 3 |
| Viva on experiments, activities and project | 5 |

### Where the 5-markers come from

Section E is three questions, and CBSE almost always spreads them across three *different* blocks —
typically one from Electrostatics/Current Electricity, one from Magnetism/EMI/AC, and one from
Optics. Each is usually "derivation + application" or "derivation + numerical".

The recurring pool:

- Ray optics: lens/mirror formula derivation, refraction through a prism, compound microscope or
  astronomical telescope (magnification + ray diagram)
- Wave optics: Young's double slit — fringe width derivation; single-slit diffraction
- AC: series LCR circuit with phasor diagram, impedance, resonance; transformer
- EMI: AC generator, self/mutual inductance, motional emf
- Moving charges: Biot–Savart applications (loop axis), Ampere's law (solenoid/toroid),
  moving coil galvanometer, force between parallel currents
- Electrostatics: Gauss's law applications (shell, wire, sheet), capacitor with dielectric, energy stored
- Current electricity: Wheatstone bridge, metre bridge, drift velocity → Ohm's law

### Where the 4-mark case studies come from

Two case studies, four sub-parts each carrying 1 mark, or 3 + 1. The reliable sources are Ray
Optics (a lens/mirror or human-eye passage), Semiconductor Electronics (a diode/rectifier passage),
Current Electricity (cells, internal resistance) and Photoelectric Effect (a graph passage).

## 3. Deleted topics — do not study these

These were removed in the rationalised syllabus and have **not** been asked since. Verify against
the current-year CBSE syllabus PDF at the start of the session, but as of the 2025-26 and 2026-27
documents, all of the following are out.

### Mathematics

| Chapter | Deleted |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Relations and Functions | Composition of functions, invertible functions, binary operations |
| 2. Inverse Trigonometric Functions | Graphs of inverse trig functions, elementary properties (the `2 tan⁻¹x` identities etc.) |
| 3. Matrices | Elementary row/column operations, inverse by elementary operations, existence of non-zero matrices whose product is the zero matrix, proof of uniqueness of inverse |
| 4. Determinants | Properties of determinants (the evaluate-by-properties questions), minors/cofactors as a standalone topic beyond what is needed for adjoint |
| 5. Continuity & Differentiability | Rolle's theorem, Lagrange's Mean Value Theorem and their geometric interpretation |
| 6. Application of Derivatives | Tangents and normals, approximations, rate of change of *bodies* framing beyond simple rates |
| 7. Integrals | Definite integral as a limit of a sum; some special integral forms |
| 8. Application of Integrals | Area between two curves (only area under simple curves, and area between a line and a circle/parabola/ellipse, remain) |
| 9. Differential Equations | Formation of a differential equation whose general solution is given; equations reducible to homogeneous form |
| 10. Vector Algebra | Scalar triple product |
| 11. Three Dimensional Geometry | **All plane topics** — equation of a plane, angle between two planes, angle between a line and a plane, distance of a point from a plane, coplanarity of lines |
| 12. Linear Programming | Diet / transportation / manufacturing problem *types* as named categories; problems with more than three non-trivial constraints |
| 13. Probability | Variance and standard deviation of a random variable; Bernoulli trials and the Binomial distribution |

> **The two that trip students up most:** planes are gone from 3D Geometry, and the Binomial
> distribution is gone from Probability. Older question banks, YouTube playlists and second-hand
> notes are full of both. If a practice paper asks you the equation of a plane or `nCr p^r q^(n-r)`,
> it is an old paper.

### Physics

| Chapter | Deleted |
| --- | --- |
| 2. Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance | Van de Graaff generator |
| 3. Current Electricity | Potentiometer (theory **and** practical), carbon resistors and colour code |
| 4. Moving Charges and Magnetism | Cyclotron |
| 5. Magnetism and Matter | Magnetic susceptibility and permeability, hysteresis, electromagnets and permanent magnets, para/dia/ferro treated quantitatively |
| 7. Alternating Current | LC oscillations |
| 10. Wave Optics | Resolving power of microscope and telescope, validity of ray optics, polarisation by scattering |
| 13. Nuclei | Radioactivity — alpha, beta, gamma decay, decay law, half-life and mean life |
| 14. Semiconductor Electronics | Zener diode and voltage regulator, optoelectronic devices (LED, photodiode, solar cell), transistors, transistor as amplifier/oscillator, logic gates |

> **Consequence for Ch 14:** what remains is small and highly predictable — energy bands, intrinsic
> vs extrinsic semiconductors, p-n junction formation, diode I–V characteristics, and diode as a
> half-wave / full-wave rectifier. Seven marks, five topics. Learn it in a weekend.

## 4. The marking scheme, and how marks are actually awarded

This is the part no textbook tells you.

**CBSE marks answers against a step-list, not against the final answer.** The marking scheme for a
5-mark derivation might read: diagram ½, statement of the law ½, setting up the expression 1,
algebra 2, final result 1. Which means:

1. **A correct final answer with no working scores a fraction of the marks.** Show the steps.
2. **A wrong final answer with correct method scores most of the marks.** Never leave a numerical
   blank because you cannot finish it — write the formula, substitute, and go as far as you can.
3. **Diagrams and labels carry independent marks.** A ray diagram in Optics, a phasor diagram in AC,
   a circuit diagram, a feasible-region graph in LP, a shaded-area sketch in Application of
   Integrals — each is worth ½ to 1 mark *on its own*, awarded even if the rest is wrong. Draw it
   even when the question does not explicitly say "draw".
4. **Units carry marks in Physics.** A numerical answer without a unit typically loses ½ mark. So
   does an answer with the wrong unit.
5. **Vector answers must look like vectors.** In Maths, if the answer is a vector and you write it
   without the arrow/î ĵ k̂ form, expect to lose the last mark.
6. **"State" vs "Derive" vs "Explain" are different instructions.** *State* wants the statement, one
   or two lines, no derivation — do not waste eight minutes deriving. *Derive* wants every algebraic
   step. *Explain* wants reasoning in words plus, usually, a diagram.

**Time budget.** 3 hours for 80 marks (Maths) is ~2 min/mark with 20 minutes spare; 3 hours for 70
marks (Physics) is ~2.3 min/mark with 25 minutes spare. Spend the 15-minute reading time marking
which internal-choice option you will attempt, and which order you will attempt sections in.

**Order to attempt.** Not 1 → 38. Do the section you are strongest in first to bank marks and settle
nerves — for most students that is Section A (MCQs) then Section D/E (the long ones, while you are
fresh), then B and C. Attempt every numerical you have a formula for, even partially.

## 5. Answer-sheet discipline

- Write the question number in the margin, exactly as on the paper. Marks are lost every year to
  mis-numbered answers.
- If you attempt a question and then attempt the internal-choice alternative too, **cross out the
  one you don't want**. Otherwise the examiner marks the first one, whichever that was.
- Start each answer on a new line, not a new page — but do leave two blank lines between answers.
- Box or underline every final answer.
- Never use pencil except for diagrams and graphs.
- Rough work goes on the last page, marked "Rough work" — not in the margins.
