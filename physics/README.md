## Unit weightage

CBSE publishes Physics weightage in **paired units** — that pairing is itself information, because
the board balances marks across the pair rather than chapter by chapter.

| Units | Chapters | Marks | Share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| V + VI — EM Waves, Optics | 8, 9, 10 | **18** | 26% |
| III + IV — Magnetism, EMI, AC | 4, 5, 6, 7 | 17 | 24% |
| I + II — Electrostatics, Current Electricity | 1, 2, 3 | 16 | 23% |
| VII + VIII — Dual Nature, Atoms, Nuclei | 11, 12, 13 | 12 | 17% |
| IX — Electronic Devices | 14 | 7 | 10% |

**Two things follow immediately:**

- **Optics is the largest single block**, and Ray Optics alone typically carries ~10 of those 18
  marks. It is the last chapter you should ever leave incomplete.
- **Chapter 14 is 7 marks from a very small syllabus** (five topics, after Zener diodes, transistors
  and logic gates were deleted). It is the highest marks-per-hour chapter in the paper, and students
  skip it because it sits last in the book. That is a free 7 marks handed away.

## The order to study in

**Follow the book order.** Physics genuinely builds — potential needs field, EMI needs magnetism,
AC needs EMI, wave optics needs the idea of a wavefront. Do not reorder it the way you can reorder
Maths.

The **only** sensible exception, and it applies to everyone: **do Chapter 14 early**, not last. It is
almost independent of everything else, it is small, and it is 7 marks. Slot it in wherever you have
a spare weekend — after Chapter 3 is a good place.

If you are short of time, the priority order is in
[`../docs/study-plan.md`](../docs/study-plan.md) (Plan B and Plan C).

## Four habits specific to Physics

**1. Keep a derivations notebook, separate from your notes.** One derivation per page, done from
memory, dated. Redo each one at day 7 and day 30. A derivation you have *read* is not a derivation
you can *write* under time pressure — and 3- and 5-mark questions are mostly derivations.

**2. Every numerical gets three lines.** Formula → substitution with units → answer with unit. The
middle line is where the method marks live, and the unit on the last line is worth ½ mark on its own.

**3. Draw the diagram before the algebra.** Ray diagram, circuit, phasor diagram, field lines, energy
band diagram. It is marked independently, so it is the one part of a hard question you can always
score.

**4. Learn the graphs as objects.** Roughly a dozen graphs recur: photoelectric stopping potential vs
frequency, photocurrent vs potential, diode V–I, LCR impedance vs frequency, binding energy per
nucleon vs mass number, resistivity vs temperature, and so on. For each one, know the **shape**, the
**meaning of the slope**, and the **meaning of the intercepts** — because that is exactly what gets
asked.

## A note on the deleted topics

The rationalised syllabus removed a lot from Physics, and the removals are unusually consequential
because they gutted specific favourite questions:

| Gone | It used to be |
| --- | --- |
| Potentiometer (theory **and** practical) | A guaranteed 3–5 mark question in Ch 3 |
| Cyclotron | A standard 3-mark derivation in Ch 4 |
| Radioactivity, decay law, half-life, mean life | Most of Ch 13's numericals |
| Zener diode, LED, photodiode, solar cell, transistors, logic gates | Most of Ch 14 |
| LC oscillations | A Ch 7 derivation |
| Resolving power of microscope/telescope | A Ch 10 formula question |
| Hysteresis, susceptibility, permeability, electromagnets | Most of Ch 5's quantitative content |

The full list is in [`../docs/00-exam-blueprint.md`](../docs/00-exam-blueprint.md) §3. **Cross these
out of any old paper or question bank before you practise from it** — otherwise you will spend a
week on the cyclotron and none on rectifiers.
